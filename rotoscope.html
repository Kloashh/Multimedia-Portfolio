Rotoscope Animation

Introducing Rotoscoping and what it is

Rotoscoping is a frame-by-frame animation technique where each frame of a video is manually traced or redrawn. This labor-intensive process creates a unique "hand-drawn" effect while maintaining the precise movement and details of the original video reference.

The Rotoscoping Process

1

Select the source video and extract individual frames

The first step was choosing a video with clear movement and expressive choreography that would be suitable for rotoscoping. Using FFmpeg, the video was converted into thousands of individual image frames. These images served as the reference for tracing every movement throughout the animation.

2

Import the frames into Krita and create a tracing layer

The extracted image sequence was imported into Krita's animation workspace. A separate drawing layer was created above the original frames, allowing each frame to be manually traced while keeping the reference visible underneath. This setup made it easier to maintain accurate proportions and smooth movement.

3

Trace each frame and export the completed drawings

Every frame was carefully redrawn by hand using Krita's animation tools. The tracing process required consistency in line work, proportions, and motion across the entire sequence. After completing the animation, the finished drawings were exported as another sequence of individual image files.

4

Compile the image sequence into a video using FFmpeg

Once all traced frames had been exported, FFmpeg was used again to combine the image sequence into a continuous video. This recreated the animation at the intended frame rate while preserving the quality of every individual drawing.

5

Add visual effects, lyrics, and final edits

The completed rotoscope animation was imported into a video editing application for post-production. Additional effects, text, lyrics, transitions, timing adjustments, and other finishing touches were added to improve the overall presentation and produce a polished final output.

6

Publish the finished animation on YouTube

After the final video was rendered, it was uploaded to YouTube for online viewing and portfolio presentation. The published video was then embedded into this webpage, allowing viewers to watch the completed rotoscope animation directly within the portfolio.
